Technical summary

R10 produced 10 deliverable geometry-conditioned computational candidates, exceeding the target of at least three candidates. Within the fixed 52-member panel, 41 passed USP15 positive AF2 gates, 36 also passed the non-PyRosetta interface audit, and 28 passed the USP4/USP11 same-pose selectivity challenge. Clustering at 80% sequence identity yielded 24 clusters; 10 representatives were exported and all 10 passed ProteinQC.

All exported candidates are 76 aa and cysteine-free. Each passed 3/3 USP15 dropout seeds and 6/6 paired selectivity seeds across USP4 and USP11. Maximum pairwise sequence identity was 73.68%.

Interpretation boundary: These results show compatibility with the supplied USP15 binding geometry and computational selectivity over USP4/USP11 under the same conditioned geometry. They are not independent sequence-only fold-and-dock validation and are not experimental binding, inhibition or cellular-activity evidence.

New in this version: whole-complex/hotspot views for the ten best positive seeds, downloadable PDBs, candidate physicochemical values, prospective explicit-solvent MD parameters, and SPR/MST starting conditions. These additions do not change the R10 geometry-conditioned interpretation boundary.

52 preselected members converged to 10 non-redundant representatives

The largest funnel contraction occurred at final representative export: 10 representatives spanning different ranks and sequence families were selected from 24 sequence clusters. The preceding quality stages retained broad pools: USP15 positive screening retained 78.8%, interface audit retained 87.8% of the preceding stage, and the dual-homolog selectivity challenge retained 77.8%. Thus the final set reflects stepwise convergence under fixed thresholds, not relaxed gates.

All 10 exported candidates retain positive-screen and counter-screen margins

Ranking prioritizes the weakest qualifying homolog separation margin (Min ΔiPAE), making Ranks 1–3 the recommended first experimental set. Rank 9 has the lowest mean USP15 iPAE and the largest interface ΔSASA, but a weaker selectivity margin than the top three. Positive geometry and homolog selectivity must therefore be considered together rather than selecting by one AF2 metric.

Scope, objects and metric definitions

Target scope. The primary target is USP15 DUSP, using 3T9L chain A residues 6–134; hotspots are fixed at A50, A52, A53, A55, A57 and A61. Counter-screen targets are structurally aligned USP4 DUSP (5CTR) and USP11 DUSP (4MEL).

Positive-screen metrics. iPAE is predicted interface error, with lower values preferred; target-aligned binder RMSD measures retention of the designed pose after target-chain alignment; binder pLDDT measures local binder confidence. Fixed gates are iPAE ≤10, binder RMSD ≤2 Å and binder pLDDT ≥80.

Interface metrics. Interface ΔSASA is the solvent-accessible surface area buried upon complex formation; fixed requirements are ≥600 Å², zero severe clashes below 1.5 Å, N_contact_hotspots ≥8 and N_hotspots_on_interface ≥4.

Selectivity metrics. For each homolog, at least 2/3 paired seeds must satisfy off-target iPAE − USP15 iPAE ≥5 and off-target iPAE ≥15 or binder RMSD >4 Å. Min ΔiPAE is the weakest separation margin among qualifying USP4/USP11 pairs for a candidate.

R10 uses a positive-control-calibrated geometry-conditioned protocol

R10 reuses the fixed 52-member cysteine-free panel generated by prior RFD1 partial diffusion and LigandMPNN. Positive screening uses AF2 model_2_ptm, interface-template (ct) conditions, 3 recycles and dropout seeds 0–2; a candidate must pass all three fixed positive gates in at least 2/3 seeds.

Positive-screen passers then undergo a non-PyRosetta interface audit based on Biopython SASA. USP4 and USP11 DUSP cores are aligned to USP15 while the initial binder coordinates are retained. The same model, template mode, recycle count and seeds are used for the same-pose challenge. Passing designs are clustered at 80% sequence identity and representative candidates are selected.

This round uses no PyRosetta, RFD3 or relaxed AF2/hotspot thresholds. ESM-IF1 and PyDSSP are used only for ranking and structural diagnostics, not as hard gates.

Both homologous DUSP structures were aligned at low RMSD

USP4 uses 5CTR chain C residues 10–138 and aligns to the USP15 DUSP core at 1.416 Å Cα RMSD. USP11 uses 4MEL chain B residues 35–157 and aligns at 1.444 Å Cα RMSD. These similar core-alignment errors support a same-pose compatibility comparison across the three DUSP surfaces, but do not replace direct competitive-binding experiments.

Negative calibration results limit the strength of candidate interpretation

The most important uncertainty is dependence on a supplied binding pose. R8 Boltz-2 sequence-only calibration did not recover both positive controls, and R9 AF2 target-template-only calibration failed across 3/3 seeds. R10 must therefore be interpreted as a geometry-conditioned compatibility test: it evaluates whether a supplied pose is compatible with USP15 and comparatively incompatible with USP4/USP11 under the same conditioning. It does not prove that the sequence will independently form that pose without a template.

No experimental errors or affinity values are available. iPAE, pLDDT, RMSD, ΔSASA and Protein-Sol are computational metrics and cannot be converted into KD, kon, koff, IC50, thermal stability or cellular effects.

Homolog counter-screen scope is limited. This round covers only USP4 and USP11 DUSP domains; proteome-wide nonspecific binding, full-length conformations, post-translational modifications and cellular environments are not modeled.

Robustness evidence. Every exported candidate passed 3/3 USP15 seeds and 6/6 paired USP4/USP11 selectivity seeds. All 10 passed the fixed ProteinQC gates, and maximum pairwise sequence identity remained below 80%. This reduces single-seed and redundancy risk but does not remove template-conditioning risk.

Express all 10 candidates in parallel first, then down-select stepwise by evidence

  1. Expression and monomeric state: build cleavable-tag constructs for all 10 candidates and record soluble yield, SEC monomer fraction and aggregation tendency.
  2. Direct binding: measure binding to USP15 DUSP by BLI, SPR or MST and require concentration dependence in at least one orthogonal assay.
  3. Homolog selectivity: test USP4 and USP11 DUSP domains in parallel using the same sample batch and full concentration curves.
  4. Full-length confirmation: test full-length USP15 for candidates passing the first three stages.
  5. Mechanism and cellular assays: evaluate enzyme activity, native interactions or cellular phenotypes only after direct binding and homolog selectivity are established.

If resources allow only three first-pass candidates, start with Ranks 1–3. If five are feasible, add Rank 9 (best mean USP15 iPAE/largest ΔSASA) and Rank 10 (lowest mean binder RMSD) to increase property diversity.

  • Does the binder recognize full-length USP15 or only the isolated DUSP conformation?
  • Does binding alter a native interaction or enzymatic behavior, or merely form a nonfunctional complex?
  • Should local affinity maturation focus on successful sequence families after the first experimental round?
