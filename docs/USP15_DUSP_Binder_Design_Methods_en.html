USP15 DUSP Binder Design, Simulation and Analysis Manual

Project: USP15_DUSP_R1 · Target: USP15 N-terminal DUSP domain · Purpose: methods, parameters and reproducibility

Core conclusion: The project follows a staged workflow: structural hotspot definition → RFdiffusion backbone generation → structure/sequence screening without relaxed empirical thresholds → USP4/USP11 counter-screening → explicit-solvent OpenMM molecular dynamics. Computational results are used for ranking and experiment design; they are not experimental binding, inhibition, or absolute thermodynamic evidence.

1. Target and hotspot definition

The design target is the USP15 N-terminal DUSP domain rather than full-length USP15. The primary reference is PDB 3T9L, chain A residues 6–134; waters, ligands, other chains and non-standard records are removed.

Hotspots come from the structurally resolved USP15 DUSP–UbV interface (PDB 6DJ9), not from post hoc selection in a single MD trajectory. Source-structure residue identifiers are:

A50, A52, A53, A55, A57, A61

In some standardized AF2 outputs, these map to target-chain identifiers B45, B47, B48, B50, B52 and B56. This is a numbering map, not a different set of biological hotspots.

The first-principles rule is to prioritize residues that lie at the experimental interface, can be contacted by a candidate binder, and have well-defined side-chain geometry in the coordinates. Interface-contact and interface-hotspot counts are then used as hard gates rather than replacing structural evidence with a single score.

2. Binder design workflow

① Structure preparation
3T9L A6–134, six hotspots with standardized chains and numbering.
② Backbone generation
OVO/RFdiffusion RFD1 generate 45–80 aa mini-protein backbones.
③ Sequence design
LigandMPNN/ProteinMPNN route; temperature 0.1, omit C.
④ Structure screening
interface hotspots, AF2 geometry, interface area and quality control.
⑤ Selectivity counter-screen
map the candidate pose to USP4/USP11 DUSP cores.
⑥ Dynamics validation
OpenMM ff19SB/OPC, three 100-ns replicas.

Selectivity counter-screen

The USP15 candidate pose is held fixed and mapped to the human USP4 (5CTR) and USP11 (4MEL) DUSP cores. After standardizing complex-chain identifiers, the same AF2 tests are run. For each off-target, both models must satisfy:

  • ΔiPAE ≥ 5(worse than USP15); and
  • off-target iPAE ≥ 15 or binder RMSD > 4 Å.

A design that passes positive screening but fails counter-screening is not a USP15-selective candidate.

7. Thermodynamic and energy analysis

The current analysis is a frame-wise, relative MM/GBSA diagnostic:

&gb
  igb=8, saltcon=0.150,
/
  • Implicit-solvent GB model: igb=8
  • Ionic strength: saltcon=0.150 M
  • AmberTools radii/topology treatment: mbondi3
  • sample one frame per ns after the 20-ns burn-in
  • report relative energy differences and distributions for complex, receptor and binder
Boundary: In this project MM/GBSA is used only for relative ranking and diagnostics under one protocol. It must not be interpreted as an absolute binding free energy or converted to KD, kon or koff. Rigorous thermodynamic quantities require a separate alchemical free-energy, umbrella-sampling, metadynamics or PMF design with convergence and error analysis.

8. Limitations and experimental follow-up

  1. Structure prediction, interface scoring and MD are model-dependent computational evidence.
  2. Unrestrained bound-state MD can test retention of a constructed complex over the simulated time window, but cannot prove spontaneous association from unbound states.
  3. To observe association, use separated states, multiple independent replicas and an unrestrained association protocol; report non-observation honestly.
  4. SPR, MST, ITC or enzyme assays should use computational results for candidate ranking, not treat computational thresholds as experimental positivity criteria.
  5. Before all 30 replicas are complete, do not make overall stability, selectivity or thermodynamic claims.
